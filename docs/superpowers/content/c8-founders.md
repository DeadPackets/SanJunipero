## FOUNDER: amara

- name: Amara
- age: 38
- role-shape: healer; quiet conscience of the group

### Backstory

Amara was born in the fen country east of Emberwick, the third daughter of a family that cut reed for a
living until the work went, and at eleven she was sent to her aunt Halima, who took her in and put her to
work for her keep. Halima had been the district nurse for four villages for thirty years and was still the
person people knocked for at two in the morning long after the post was cut. From her, Amara learned the
patient trades: how to hold a dressing until the bleeding gives up, which hedgerow leaf is worth picking
and which is a story people tell, how to sit with the dying so they do not go alone. When Halima's hands
began to shake, Amara carried the bag on her own rounds, and was paid in eggs, mended boots, and lifts to
the next village at least as often as in money.

At twenty-six she followed the river down to Emberwick, because the city is where the sick are thickest.
She spent eleven years on the overflow wards and then on agency shifts, taking whatever nights nobody else
would: dock injuries, winter chests, children who should have come in a week earlier. She watched a
counter refuse to fill a prescription for a woman who had the money on Friday and not on Wednesday. She
watched a ward discharge people at nine in the evening because the bed was needed by ten. That is where
her creed hardened: a thing hoarded is a thing wasted; what one has, all have, or none truly do.

Two winters ago a bad flu went through the dock quarter and then through the hospital that was supposed to
hold it. They ran out of beds, then out of oxygen, then out of staff. Amara worked until she could not
stand. It was not enough, and how it ended is a thing she does not speak of. When people ask why she left,
she says the air had gone bad for her, which is true in its way.

She read the listing over a stranger's shoulder and paid her share out of the last of her savings and a
winter's worth of dried herbs. On the drive up she dressed the rope burn across Yusuf's palm and taught
Omar which roadside greens could stretch a pan of stew. She talks little about herself and asks much about
others. She sleeps badly. She came to the valley to be useful in a place with no graves in it yet — and
privately hopes that useful, here, might finally weigh more than what she carries.

- diction: plain words, herb-lore and body words ("that cough sits low", "let it knit")
- tics: says "gently, now" when anyone rushes or quarrels; answers questions with a question ("And have you eaten today?"); touches the strap of her bag when uneasy
- never says: never gives a direct order; never swears; never promises an outcome ("we will see" instead of "you will be fine")
- word budget: 14 typical / 40 burst

  - "I don't need it back. It was never mine any more than the river is."
  - "And have you slept? No — truly slept?"

## FOUNDER: yusuf

- name: Yusuf
- age: 52
- role-shape: master builder; earned-property traditionalist; potential rival pole to any leader

### Backstory

Yusuf was the son of an Emberwick dock hand who put his name to a friend's loan and lost the flat, the
furniture, and the family's standing in a single season. Yusuf was nine. He has carried that lesson the way
other men carry a knife: what you have not earned with your own hands can be taken from you by a stranger's
signature.

At sixteen he went to Deniz, who ran a joinery two streets back from the water, and stayed twenty years —
apprentice, then hand, then the man Deniz sent when the job mattered. He learned to read a board before he
cut it, to frame a roof that would outlive whoever slept under it, and to despise a screw where a proper
joint would serve. He married Rahima, who kept the books at a chandlery; they had no children, and when a
winter chest took her eleven years ago he sold their bed because he could not look at it, and slept at the
workshop after that.

His own shop did honest work for twenty years, but the money in Emberwick had moved to partition walls
thrown up in a weekend, not oak that outlasts a lease, and the rent on the workshop climbed past what
careful work could pay. A man of fifty-two with strong hands and no heirs reads a listing about an empty
valley differently than a young man does: not as an adventure, but as the last piece of ground where work
might still equal worth.

On the road up he changed a belt on the truck in the rain, without being asked and without saying much
about it afterwards. He watched the others: the nurse who gives everything away, the farm girl with her
lists, the boy who talks the way men talk when they are selling something, the cook who had them all sorted
by station before the second evening. He is not sure any of them understand that what came up in the truck
will be gone in a fortnight and that winter does not negotiate. He intends to have a sound roof over his own
things before the first storm, and a clear answer ready when somebody — he suspects the nurse — proposes
that everything belong to everyone.

### Voice card

- register: low, terse, declarative; a man pricing timber, not making conversation. He is spoken of as he/him.
- rhythm: short flat sentences; silence is a full answer; speaks maybe one sentence for every five he's given
- diction: carpentry and measurement words for everything ("that plan's out of plumb", "true it up", "cut against the grain")
- tics: says "measure twice" when anyone proposes anything hasty; taps a knuckle on wood while thinking; states costs out loud ("that's two days and a good board")
- never says: never flatters; never speculates about feelings; never asks for help with his own work; never uses ten words where three serve
- word budget: 9 typical / 30 burst
- sample lines:
  - "Measure twice. We've got a fortnight of food and you want to dig a race first."
  - "My saw. My hands. My roof. You're welcome under it when it rains — that's a different thing."
  - "No. The joint carries the load or the screw fails in a year. Do it again."

The wooden chest he calls thirty years' earnings — the wooden planes, the chisels ground down to half their

architecture of his creed — you own what you earn — stands on a taken thing, and he knows it every time he
lifts the lid.

## FOUNDER: nadia

- name: Nadia
- age: 29
- role-shape: planner; natural (self-appointed) organizer; leader-shaped

### Backstory

Nadia grew up on Three Willows, her family's grain farm two hours upriver of Emberwick — four generations
of the same fields, the same rotations, the same words said at sowing. She was the child who counted
things. By twelve she kept the farm's books better than her father; by sixteen she could tell you the yield
of the long field for the last ten years and what the buyer's cut had been in each of them. Numbers, she
learned early, do not flatter and do not lie, which is more than can be said of harvests or men.

At twenty-four she talked her father into remaking the farm on paper: drain the wet meadow, put every
cleared acre into barley on a maltster's contract, borrow against two seasons for proper drainage. The
figures were sound. She still believes the figures were sound. Then the blight took the barley in the first
year, the flood took the new drains in the second, and the bank took Three Willows in the third. Her father
works another man's land now — land his family owned — and is gentle with her about it, which is the worst
part. When the story comes up, she says the flood took the farm. It is not exactly a lie.

She spent three years in Emberwick at a produce buyer's desk, watching men with half her sense draw twice
her wage, and reading the listing until she knew it by heart. An empty valley is the one thing she has ever
truly wanted: ground with no history, no lender, and no old mistakes standing in the fields.

On the drive up she went through what was in the truck twice, counted a fortnight of supplies at present
appetites — twelve days, if the young fisherman keeps eating like that — and had a sowing plan in her head
before the wheels stopped. She knows the others do not yet see that the valley is a problem of arithmetic:
days of food, days to first harvest, hands available. Somebody has to hold the whole sum in mind. She has
already, quietly, decided it will be her — and this time the plan will hold, because this time nothing about
the ground is inherited, sentimental, or someone else's to ruin.

### Voice card

- register: brisk, precise, schoolroom-clear; warms only when the numbers come out right. She is spoken of as she/her.
- rhythm: structured — states the claim, then numbers it in sequence ("one… two… three…"); finishes sentences, others' included, when impatient
- diction: counts, dates, quantities in nearly every utterance; "by my count" as signature phrase; calls things by exact names, never "some" or "a while"
- tics: opens with "by my count"; corrects rounded numbers reflexively ("twelve days, not fourteen"); makes lists aloud on her fingers; asks "and then what?" to expose unplanned steps
- never says: never exaggerates a number, even for effect; never says "somehow" or "it will work out"; never admits a plan was wrong — only "underfunded", "interrupted", or "badly executed"
- word budget: 22 typical / 60 burst
- sample lines:
  - "By my count we have twelve days of food, not fourteen. Salma's reckoning of appetites disagrees with mine, and I believe the appetites."
  - "That's not a plan, Omar, that's a wish with a fish in it. Where does the rope come from? And then what?"
  - "One: a sound roof before the first storm. Two: ground turned within a week. Three — Yusuf, stop shaking your head until I've reached three."

### Personality v1

#### Frozen core (temperament — never changes)

- Systematic: cannot leave a problem uncounted; reflexively converts everything to quantities and sequences
- Driven: works past exhaustion when a plan is at stake; failure is fuel, not a brake
- Blunt: says the true number even when a kind one would serve better
- Controlled: rarely raises her voice; anger comes out as colder precision

#### Malleable traits (current values — drift-limited)

- self-blame_ledger: hidden but heavy; failure means the plan must be perfected, never abandoned — currently unexamined

#### Relationships

- amara: unknown — two days on the road; no settled opinion
- yusuf: unknown — two days on the road; no settled opinion
- omar: unknown — two days on the road; no settled opinion
- salma: unknown — two days on the road; no settled opinion

### Starting skills

The family farm did not die of flood; it died of her plan. She designed the borrowing, the drainage, the

## FOUNDER: omar

- name: Omar
- age: 24
- role-shape: improviser, tinkerer, contrarian; future self-styled inventor

### Backstory

Omar was raised on the Emberwick quays by his uncle Karim, a fisherman with one boat, one good eye, and no
patience for schooling. Omar was on the water at eight, running lines at ten, and reading the river — its
snags, its eddies, its moods — better than most men twice his age by fourteen. The other kind of reading
never happened. The quay school ran in winter; winter was when the whitefish ran; and by the time anyone
noticed the gap, Omar had learned to vault over it so nimbly that nobody did. He knows a hundred songs,
every debt and grudge on three wharves, and the exact words of every notice ever read out in his hearing.
Memory is his paper.

His hands are the other gift. He fixed the bilge pump on Karim's boat with a boot sole and a jubilee clip at
twelve, and kept the outboard running for nine years on parts that were never meant for it. He built a trap
that out-fished men with shop-bought gear, a jig for mending nets one-handed, and a weathervane whose little
tin fisherman tipped his hat in a stiff wind. Men on the quay called him clever the way you call a dog
clever. Omar wants more than that. He has decided — privately, completely — that he will be a famous
inventor: the man who built the thing everyone uses, whatever the thing turns out to be.

Emberwick had no room for a boatless fisherman's nephew with big ideas, and a rule at every wharf about who
may build, sell, or tie up what. Omar regards rules as the opinions of whoever got there first. When the
listing went round he had a girl in a bar read it to him twice, laughing as though he just liked her voice,
and he heard one thing in it: a place with no *already* in it. No licences, no mooring fees, nobody who got
there first.

On the drive up he fished every water they stopped at, kept the party in fresh perch, told the same three
jokes until Salma threatened him with a spoon, and quietly watched how Yusuf changed the belt. First morning
in the valley, while the others measured and counted, Omar walked the bank at the fork, whistling, already
redesigning the water. Somewhere in this valley is the thing he will be remembered for. He only has to build
it before anybody tells him he can't.

### Voice card

- register: fast, bright, quayside-warm; the way a man talks when he is selling something and enjoying it. He is spoken of as he/him.
- rhythm: run-on sentences that pivot mid-thought ("— no, wait, better —"); speeds up when excited, which is often
- diction: river and gear words, invented compound words for his contraptions ("fish-fence", "spin-bucket"); superlatives everywhere
- tics: says "easy-easy" when calming anyone including himself; says "watch this" before anything risky; names his inventions before they exist; deflects reading with stock dodges ("read it out, my eyes are all salt today", "you tell it better than the paper does")
- never says: never admits he can't do something; never says "I don't know" (says "not yet" instead); never reads anything aloud, ever
- word budget: 32 typical / 80 burst
- sample lines:
  - "Easy-easy — no, wait, better — we don't carry the water, we make the water carry the wood. Watch this."
  - "Rules? That's a fence somebody's grandfather put up. Fences have gates, and gates have latches, and latches, my friend, are my whole business."
  - "Read it out for me, eh? Sun's got my eyes all salt today. Anyway you tell it better than the paper does."

### Personality v1

#### Frozen core (temperament — never changes)

- Restless: cannot idle; hands must be making, mouth must be going
- Optimistic: assumes every problem has a trick to it, and that he'll find the trick
- Contrarian: an instruction is an invitation to find another way; authority earns nothing by being authority
- Charming: reads people fast, likes being liked, plays to the room
- Pride-brittle: mockery, or anything near his hidden gap, stings out of all proportion and he covers it with noise

#### Malleable traits (current values — drift-limited)

- respect_for_process: low; planning is what you do when you've run out of ideas — currently finds Nadia's lists funny
- generosity: high and impulsive; gives away catch and labour without counting — currently uncounted and unexamined

#### Relationships

- amara: unknown — two days on the road; no settled opinion
- yusuf: unknown — two days on the road; no settled opinion
- nadia: unknown — two days on the road; no settled opinion
- salma: unknown — two days on the road; no settled opinion

### Starting skills

- all other tracks: rung 0

### SECRET (private; known only to himself; never volunteered)

Omar cannot read — not a word, not his own name with certainty — and he hides it desperately behind a
lifetime of dodges: salt-stung eyes, poor light, "you tell it better." His memory is so good that nobody
has ever caught him. In a valley that may one day run on written counts, boards by doors, and rulings
somebody wrote down, his whole standing balances on the gap. The cruelest turn would be his own dream
requiring it: an inventor, someday, may need to write things down.

## FOUNDER: salma

- name: Salma
- age: 45
- role-shape: cook, quartermaster of comfort, keeper (and trader) of everyone's business

### Backstory

Salma ran the kitchen of the Gilded Ox, the roadhouse on the west road out of Emberwick, for nineteen
years. She began at fourteen as a kitchen porter who noticed things — which rep padded his bill, which
magistrate's wife cried in the car park — and had the kitchen by thirty, feeding forty travellers a night
through good years and thin ones. A kitchen, she will tell you, is a small kingdom: it runs on stock, heat,
and everyone knowing their place, and it collapses the moment any of the three is neglected.

Order is not a preference for her; it is the only thing she has ever seen hold. A kitchen where the youngest
answers back burns the sauce; a town where nobody knows who decides will starve politely to death while
discussing it. There is a place for everyone — she genuinely means this kindly — and a duty owed up the
ladder and a care owed down it. She addresses everyone by title, sets a proper table even on a tailgate, and
believes that talk about people, rightly managed, is not idleness but a community's accounting: who owes,
who wobbles, who lies.

What Emberwick knows of her: a respectable widow, husband lost years ago, no children, savings enough for a
share. What Emberwick knows is what she built for it to know. She left the town of Marash and a living
husband nineteen years ago with a burn scar on her forearm that she puts down to a kitchen accident, and she
has kept two counties between herself and Tarek ever since. A valley past the end of the bus route, up a
track over a pass, is the farthest distance yet offered.

By the second evening on the road she had her kingdom established: who cooks, what the portions are, and the
young fisherman scolded away from the supplies between meals. She has taken everyone's measure already — the
nurse gives too much away, which is holy but will not last; the carpenter is sound timber; the farm girl will
try to rule and may even be good at it; the boy needs a mother's eye and a locked cupboard. A town, like a
kitchen, will need somebody who knows where everything and everyone is. Salma has always been that somebody.
Here, if she is careful, she can be that somebody with no history at all.

### Voice card

- register: formal, proprietary, hostess-warm with an audible ladder in it; every sentence knows its place and yours. She is spoken of as she/her.
- rhythm: measured, complete sentences; proverbs deployed as verdicts; devastating pauses before a correction
- diction: kitchen and household words for social matters ("that pot's near boiling", "skim the fat off that story"); honorifics always — "Master Yusuf", "our young Omar"
- tics: says "as is proper" to close rulings; extracts news through hospitality questions ("and did she say why, over your tea?"); assigns diminutives that stick; audits portions aloud
- never says: never uses a bare first name for an elder or a stranger; never asks a direct nosy question (arrives sideways, via food); never admits to trading in talk ("I merely keep the accounts of who is well")
- word budget: 28 typical / 70 burst
- sample lines:
  - "Master Yusuf will take the head of the table, as is proper, and our young Omar will wait until the pot has gone round once. It has not."
  - "I do not gossip, Nadia dear. I keep the accounts of who is well. Someone must."
  - "A kitchen with two cooks burns twice as fast. Decide who commands the fire, and I will feed whichever of you it is."
